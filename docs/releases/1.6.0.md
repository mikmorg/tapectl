# tapectl 1.6.0: release notes

What changed between `v1.5.0` and 1.6.0, for the person who runs tapectl. The record is
`git log v1.5.0..` on the 1.6.0 tree, issues #420 and #488, and ADR-0012 items 110-120.

- **A tape that reaches the drive's early warning seals short instead of failing** (#420). When
  the drive reports the end of its medium during a data slice, the write finishes that slice,
  writes a *short seal* after it and stops. Every unit wholly before the warning is on the tape
  and counts as a copy. The units after it are *cut*: they stay staged, and the next ordinary
  `tapectl volume write` writes them whole onto another cartridge. A unit is never split. Before
  1.6.0 the same warning aborted the write and left the tape unsealed.
- **Every 1.6.0 tape fences out older readers** (item 111). File 0 states
  `requires = ["short-seal"]` on every tape, full or short, so a 1.5.0 RESTORE.sh or tapectl
  refuses it by name instead of half-reading it. 1.6.0 reads every older tape as before.
- **A write may plan past the fill ceiling** (item 113), up to 100% of the cartridge's nominal
  capacity, when the drive declares `post_warning_bytes`. Whole units are added in order; the
  ones past the ceiling are named before the tape moves ("planned past the fill ceiling; the
  tape may end before them"). Without the key, plans stop at the fill ceiling as before.
- **A write that saw the early warning reads back what it wrote past it** (item 114): the slice
  in flight and the seal, under every confirm tier, so a block the drive kept after refusing it
  is caught.
- **Write shaping, the first build** (#488): a faster hash (ring), the HP drive's own wear
  counter in every write's feed-ratio record, and an opt-in trace of the write's feed.
- **One catalog migration, 038.**
- **Issues this release finishes:** #420. #488's first build ships; its drive phase and its
  shaping decisions are still open.

## On-tape changes

The golden pins moved under ADR-0012 item 116 (the CTO pre-authorized exactly the moves items
111-115 name): RESTORE.sh `95862b49…` → `d9787a4f…`, MANIFEST.toml by one line. A third pin was
added for the short seal's bytes (`tests/on_tape_golden.rs`).

- **File 0** states `requires = ["short-seal"]` on the line after `layout_version`, on every
  tape. Nothing else in it changed.
- **File 3, the front index,** states `may_end_early = true` in `[index]`, with a paragraph
  saying the tape may end before the last file it plans and that the seal says where.
- **MANIFEST.toml** (every envelope) states `may_end_early = true` after `created_at`.
- **The seal marker.** A full seal now states `requires = ["short-seal"]` and
  `ended_after = file_count - 2`. A *short seal* is the same document written after file K, the
  last file on the tape: `file_count = K + 2`, `ended_after = K`, and its copy of the front index
  holds entries 0 to K and its own entry. Its header is identical to a full seal's
  (`volume-format-v2.md` §4.1, parse rules S1-S6).
- **Where the seal is** (§1.2 rule 6): the last file at END OF DATA, counted by filemarks. A
  reader that knows `ended_after` from a catalog reads it at K+1 directly.
- **RECOVERY.md** gains "Where this tape ended", with the four hand-read lines for the seal.
  **File 1** gains "Where the tape ended", and its glossary entry for `requires` is rewritten.
- **RESTORE.sh** knows `short-seal`, and its "needs" text names tapectl 1.6.0. On a 1.6.0 tape
  it reads the seal right after the front index (one wind to the end of the data and back),
  keeps its file map to files 0 to K and:
  - `--info` says SEALED and where the tape ended, and marks the planned files after it NOT ON
    TAPE;
  - `--verify` passes files 0 to K and prints NOT ON TAPE for the rest (exit 0);
  - `--restore` of a cut unit is refused by name, naming `--where`; `--restore --all` restores
    the whole units and names the cut ones;
  - `--list` adds "NOT ON THIS TAPE WHOLE" for a cut unit; `--where` and `--find` mark this
    tape's cut versions; `--fill-from` refuses a cut unit; `--swap-tapes` reads the second
    tape's seal first.
  A 1.5.0 tape reads exactly as before.

## tapectl changes

**`volume write` and `volume resume`:**
- Past the early warning st refuses every other block with ENOSPC. The writer retries a refused
  block; more than 16 refusals in a row is the medium's hard end, which aborts as before.
- The write commits a `write_early_warning` event (field `ended_after`, details `bytes_at`,
  `refusals`, `post_warning_bytes`, `seal`).
- On a short seal it prints, and exits 0:
  ```
  volume "SE-1" sealed short: the drive's early warning came in tape file 9, so the tape ends after it. Every unit wholly before it is on this tape and counts as a copy. Cut (not on this tape, still staged): b, c. Write them whole onto another cartridge with an ordinary `tapectl volume write <label>` (`--init` initialises a new one).
  ```
  `write_completed`'s details name the ending and the cut units; `volume write --json` gains
  `ending`, `ended_after` and `cut_units`.
- A warning before the first data slice aborts the session. So does the medium's end while the
  seal itself is written (exit 5, nothing left to resume).
- A crash after the warning resumes to a short seal without writing another slice.
- The contact check finds a short-sealed cartridge as sealed: `--force` cannot overwrite it
  (ADR-0003).

**Confirm and `volume verify`:** the chain walk covers files 0 to `ended_after`. A seal whose
`ended_after` disagrees with the catalog's is `ended_after_disagrees`, inconclusive (exit 3),
and never quarantines, whatever else the walk found; every finding is still reported, and each
line of `volume verify` says "not medium evidence".

**Planning:** `volume plan` and `collection plan|run` show the packing figure and the units past
the fill ceiling (`collection` JSON: `packing_bytes`, `fill_bytes`, `past_ceiling`). A plan
beyond 100% of nominal is refused (`PlanBoundExceeded`), never trimmed, and so is a plan whose
first unit is already past the ceiling. `volume rewrite` never plans past the fill ceiling: it
may hold a unit's only copy.

**The catalog:** a cut unit's rows are `cut` (migration 038), never a copy, never pending, never
resumable. `volumes.ended_after` records where a short tape ended (NULL on a full one).
- `stage create --version` and the release gate name the volume that cut a unit
  (`ReleaseBlocker::Cut`), and staging is released once the version is written whole elsewhere.
- `restore unit|file|volume` refuses a cut unit by name and names the volume that holds it whole;
  `restore volume` restores the whole units and names the cut ones; `restore raw-volume` reports
  the positions after K as not on the tape. A cut write's slices are never a repair source.
- `catalog rebuild --from-volume` of a short tape gives the rows the write gave, and records
  `ended_after` on a row that lost it (a fact about the tape, never a status). The adoption of a
  lost write (`volume resume`, #360) works on a short tape.
- `volume rewrite` with a short destination stays open with the cut sets held and names
  `volume write`. **Changed for every tape:** a resumed rewrite whose destination is sealed is
  finished (read back, then the holds lifted), where before it could be closed as abandoned once
  its staging was released.
- `audit` has a new check, `short_seal`: `cut_without_ending` and `completed_past_ending`.

**#488, write shaping (first build):**
- The write, verify and staging hash is ring's SHA-256 (about 1.35x sha2's on home2's CPU, which
  has no SHA instructions). Digests are identical.
- `write_feed_ratio` events gain `repositions_per_100mb`, decoded from the HP drive's page 0x34
  (tape direction changes per 100 MB written; absent on another vendor's drive). `report events`,
  `volume info` and `report health` show it, decoding older events from the log-page journal.
- `TAPECTL_PIPELINE_TRACE=1` records `<session>.pipeline.tsv` beside the session log: one row
  every 250 ms of the queue, the threads' waits and CPU, the host's load and the staging disks.
  It changes nothing the write does.
- A build with the cargo feature `research` (never a production build) reads the
  `TAPECTL_EXP_*` knobs of `docs/research/2026-10-20-write-shaping.md` §9.1. Any other build
  ignores them and says so once.

## Tooling (not in the binary)

- `scripts/short-seal-acceptance.sh` and `examples/short_seal_filler.rs`: the 1.6.0 acceptance
  run on the real drive (items 110, 115, 117; `docs/lto6-validation-checklist.md`).
- `TAPECTL_TEST_EARLY_WARNING_AT=<bytes>` is a test hook: the write store refuses blocks as past a
  drive's early warning. Never set it.
- home2-prep.sh §9 and the operator guide say which priority settings reach what on home2 (#488
  decision 9).

## Catalog migrations

- **038:** the write status `cut`, and `volumes.ended_after`. It rebuilds the `writes` table. The
  operator envelope's `catalog.db` reads `user_version` 38.

## Testing

- The mhvtl gate has 52 checks: five `heir_short_*` legs run RESTORE.sh on a short tape with the
  real `age`, `dar`, `mt` and `tar`.
- `tests/format_v2.rs` has a keyless short-sealed harness; `tests/mhvtl_e2e.rs` writes a short
  seal over real st and proves a seal lost past the cartridge's end never seals.
- mhvtl now gives st's early-warning ENOSPC near capacity, but still loses data past it
  (measured 2026-10-20): it shows the warning, never what a drive keeps past it. The acceptance
  run on the real drive proves that part.

## Costs

- **RESTORE.sh on a 1.6.0 tape** winds to the end of the data and back before its first slice,
  full tape or short (§4.2 R1). `--info` keeps its one rewind.
- **`restore raw-volume`** on a 1.6.0 tape: the same wind (its budget moved from 1.05 to 3.05
  tape lengths).
- **The contact check** reads the last file on the medium when END OF DATA comes before the
  planned seal (at most `SMALL_FILE_CAP`).
- **The quick confirm after a warning** reads one more file, the slice at K: still one rewind and
  two forward spaces.

## Upgrading

home2 goes from 1.5.0 to 1.6.0 by `docs/handoff.md` (g), after the acceptance run passes. In
short:
- back up the catalog with 1.5.0;
- install;
- migration 038 applies at the first open;
- add `post_warning_bytes = "99008M"` to the drive's backend (item 118);
- regenerate the Heir Kit, whose copy of tapectl must read 1.6.0 tapes (item 111).

There is no downgrade past migration 038.
