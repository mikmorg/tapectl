# Adversarial review of the 1.1 diff — 2026-10-15 (#452)

**Range:** `cf4a371..d165ef7` (`v1.1.1`): the 1.1.0 and 1.1.1 releases, 214 files, about
111k inserted lines. The four earlier reviews (`2026-09-17` to `2026-09-23`) end at
`cf4a371`; nothing since had been read adversarially as code. Ruled by the CTO on
2026-10-15 (ADR-0012 item 65), run the same day after `v1.1.1` was tagged.

**Method.** Seven read-only finders (sonnet), one per subsystem, each told to trace a
defect to its end or drop it, to default to "not a defect", and to report what it read
and did not reach. Their shared rules are reproduced at the end. Six lenses first: the
write session and consent; the on-tape format and heir path; staging; the catalog,
migrations and policy; tape I/O and forensics; the scripts, harness and tests that cannot
fail. A seventh was sent to what the six reported as not reached (the readback and
recovery paths, the overlapped write pipeline, `collection plan`, escrow coverage, binding
and rebuild's cartridge binding): the completeness pass. No workflow was used. The
coordinator read the cited lines of the high and of every medium before filing; lows were
filed on the finders' traced evidence, with a spot check where the claim was cheap to read.

**Result: 66 raw findings, 64 distinct, 1 high.** Filed as 18 issues (#458–#475), four
findings as comments on existing issues (plus two cross-references), 5 recorded here only. None was refuted on verification: the
finders dropped their own doubtful cases first (each report has a "considered and
dropped" list, 6 to 15 items long), which is where the earlier reviews' 50–60% refutation
happened. The 1.1.2 queue (label `1.1.2`) now holds 30 issues.

## The high

**#458 — `catalog rebuild --from-volume` records a tape with no readable seal marker as a
sealed volume, so a never-sealed tape counts as a copy.** ADR-0012's consequences list and
#158's own ruling say a rebuild asserts `sealed` only after reading the seal marker. #158
landed with the hardcoded `'sealed'` left in place, reasoning that "the rebuild proved that
exact tape complete", which the rebuild module's own doc denies. An aborted or interrupted
write, rebuilt after a catalog loss, then counts in `audit` and every copy floor. No
production exposure: home2's two tapes are sealed and fully verified.

**The lesson is about review, not code.** The ruling was right and was written into the
issue as "the fix"; the landing reasoned it away in a comment, and integration accepted
the comment. A landing note that contradicts the ruling it closes is a finding in itself.

## Recorded only

- **Format 7.** Rebuild and `restore raw-volume` have no fallback to the seal's embedded
  front-index copy; RESTORE.sh does. A damaged File 3 restores by RESTORE.sh but cannot be
  rebuilt. A feature, not a defect against any ruling.
- **Catalog 9.** Migration 030's list of NULLed mtimes goes through `tracing::warn!`,
  subject to the log level. home2 started fresh, so no catalog it holds ran 030 on old
  data.
- **Tape 8.** The drive lock is per home and per node basename; item 29 accepts that the
  journal holds what a poll reads. The guide should state the limit.
- **Readback 7.** `bind_late` can displace without a witnessed serial, through the
  `operator_serial` fallback. Reachable only from a pre-#192 unbound row.
- **Readback, small.** A `--quick` verify after an interrupted full one makes the full
  one's checkpoints non-latest, so the next full verify reads everything again: time,
  not a verdict.

## What held

The finders traced these and found them as ruled: no plaintext archive byte reaches the
staging device, and the slice cutter is dar's own framing, proven against `dar_xform` at
1M, 4M, 1G and 10G; each slice is synced before its row; the release gate's per-unit,
least-covered-version rule; the single source read's stat rechecks and pacing; the forward-
only RESTORE.sh navigation (three motions for a unit) and its `dar_at_least` comparison;
envelope backups as byte copies and both readers' fallback; `catalog.db` generation
detection by shape; every restore path comparing the ciphertext hash; the isolation
invariant on 1.1's new plaintext fields; 0x2E read once per contact and `--maxlen` on the
one argv; the st/SG boundary; the verify verdict split (an unreadable slice is
inconclusive, a mismatch quarantines); escrow coverage never reading `unknown` as
`covered`; the cartridge serial written once; migrations 026, 027, 030, 032 and 033
restating every column, index and CHECK with `foreign_key_check`; single derivation
through `policy::coverage`; `config set` validating before an atomic replace; home
resolution; `--auto` unable to erase or overwrite a production cartridge without a
flag-supplied word.

## Finder rules (as given)

Read-only (no cargo, scripts, tapectl or `/dev`); grep then read ranges in the large
files; the design over the code, and a behaviour an ADR item rules is not a defect; high =
a sealed tape overwritten without consent, a copy counted that is not one, a destructive
step below its ADR-0008 tier, an heir silently given partial data, plaintext on tape or
staging, a bricked or silently shrunk catalog; medium = an operator told something false
at a decision point, a safety path with no test that can fail, a wrong refusal, lost
forensic evidence; each finding traced (state → command → wrong outcome) or marked
CONCERN; "considered and dropped", "read" and "not reached" lists in every report. The
shared secondary lens: every remedy string checked against the command's own refusal
logic in that state.
