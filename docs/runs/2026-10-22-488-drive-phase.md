# 2026-10-22: #488's write-shaping drive phase on home2

`scripts/shaping-drive-phase.sh` on the real drive, on the CTO's go-ahead (the write-shaping
note's §12 item 2; ADR-0012 item 117). It ran from 2026-10-22 21:22:24 to 2026-10-23 00:18:19
UTC and **passed all 14 steps**. The production home, the production binary and every
production cartridge were left alone. The protocol is the note's §10
(`docs/research/2026-10-20-write-shaping.md`).

**In short:** no load reproduced #426's ratio of about 1.5. The highest ratio was 1.0943. The
three runs that crossed 1.01 each came with a jump in the drive's own write-correction
counters (page 0x02). In those runs the feed was as steady as in the clean ones, and the drive
reported no repositions. ring's SHA-256 is the one lever the figures support. The pace made
nothing better. The feed-ratio warning blamed the host in all three runs, which is not what the
drive's pages show.

## Conditions

- **Binary:** the research build, `tapectl 1.6.0 (v1.6.0-20-gb660a26, 2026-10-22)`, a static
  musl release build with `--features research`, for measuring only (item 117). sha256
  `5bf51d0adf5e2ae2e9f280b0d18a36d9af923baaf6158d72778bb9d9f85102c2`, in
  `~/tapectl-1.6.0-research-b660a26/` on home2 with the script beside it. It was never in `PATH`
  or a production home.
- **Drive:** the HP LTO-6 HUJ808A5L4, `/dev/tape/by-id/scsi-HUJ808A5L4-nst` (`/dev/nst0`,
  `/dev/sg13`). The cartridge stayed loaded for the whole phase.
- **Cartridge:** the test cartridge, chip serial **EW7VWMVKF6**.
- **Data:** one 20 GiB synthetic set (21,490,565,120 bytes in 1 GiB slices), staged once on md3
  (`/srv/google_copy/tapectl-shaping`) and written by each run with `--keep-staging`, plus the
  20 GB load file beside it. The set was staged as two units, the smaller of 1.5 GiB for the
  restore leg.
- **Home:** a throwaway home (`~/.cache/tapectl/shp-home-20261022`).
- **Host:** the operator stopped `tapectl-drive-poll.timer`. homorg paused its timers and
  lcsas-4c held its builds, and vm-desk1 ran nothing but the runs' own loads. `host check` named
  the same one docker process in every run.
- **Run directory:**
  `~/.cache/tapectl/shaping/shp-20261022T212223Z-EW7VWMVKF6`. Each `run-*` directory keeps the
  load's commands and times, the knobs, the session log, the pipeline trace, the post-command
  sweep, the feed-ratio event, and MAM plus pages 0x02, 0x03, 0x0c, 0x14, 0x17 and 0x34 before
  and after the write. `results.tsv` holds what is derived from them.
- **Not recorded:** `meta.txt`'s `dar=` and `script_commit=` came out empty. The script's

The ratio is native per data byte, from page 0x0c BOP to EOD; page 0x0c reports native MB in
steps of about 25 MB, which is why 1.0010 and 0.9998 alternate. "Write MB/s" is decimal MB/s
over the pipeline trace's window. "Writer waited" is the time the tape writer spent waiting
on the hasher. `sha2` is the hash before 1.6.0 (the knob restores it). `ring` is what 1.6.0
ships. Setup took 11m 55s. P0a took 6m 17s and each later run 14-15 minutes, its erase and init included.

**The stop rule decided the shape.** R2 to R5 were to stop at the first load with a ratio of
1.3 or more, which would then be L\*. None came near, so the script took §10's fallback: R1,
R8 and R9 under L2, then the restore leg, then stop. **R0, R4, R6, R7, R10, R11 and R12 did not
run.** R0 is the operator's, R4 and R11 need the host owner, and the others need an L\*.

**The pass criterion cannot be judged as written.** It asks for a ratio of 1.05 or less under
L\* and repositions within the quiet baseline's range. There was no L\*, and every run reported
0 repositions.

## Findings

**1. No load reproduced #426.** CPU load on vm-desk1, 8 or 16 threads, gave 0.9998 to 1.0599.
Staging reads on md3 gave 0.9998. Staging writes on md3 gave 1.0943, the highest, and the one
load past the 1.05 warning threshold apart from R3. #426's write in production came to about
1.5.

**2. The excess came with the drive's write corrections, not with the feed.** These are the
page 0x02 deltas across each write:

| Run | Ratio | Errors corrected without substantial delay | Total rewrites or rereads | Total times correction algorithm processed |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| P0a | 1.0010 | 19 | 0 | 141 |
| P0b | 0.9998 | 18 | 0 | 52 |
| P0c | 0.9998 | 13 | 0 | 118 |
| R1 | 1.0010 | 9 | 0 | 47 |
| R2 | 0.9998 | 12 | 0 | 35 |
| **R3** | **1.0599** | **626** | 0 | **99,027** |
| R5r | 0.9998 | 9 | 0 | 24 |
| **R5w** | **1.0943** | **905** | 0 | **153,609** |
| R1-L2 | 1.0010 | 12 | 0 | 23 |
| R8-L2 | 1.0021 | 11 | 0 | 22 |
| **R9-L2** | **1.0154** | **143** | 0 | **20,728** |

- The three runs above 1.01 are the three runs where the correction counters rose 400-7000
  times over the clean runs. In R3 and R5w each "correction algorithm processed" stood for
  about 13 KB of extra native tape (1,290 MB over 99,027; 2,031 MB over 153,609), and in R9
  for about 16 KB.
- The feed in R3 was as steady as in the clean L2 runs. The trace shows no writer stall over
  100 ms, a longest gap between tape writes of 34.5 ms, and no disk await over 100 ms after
  the first sample. R3's one 494 ms disk await was that first sample, before any slice was
  written. P0b and R1-L2, with the same knobs and the same load, have the same trace and
  ratios of 0.9998 and 1.0010.
- No TapeAlert flag was set. The drive read 40 C, and page 0x14 shows two lifetime cleaning
  operations and 97-98 head hours. "Total rewrites or rereads" did not move in any run.
- Every run wrote the same first 21.5 GB of the same cartridge, from BOT.

**What makes the drive correct more on some writes is not established.** The load, the knobs
and the tape region do not separate the high runs from the clean ones. A dirty head, the
medium's wear (this cartridge's first wraps have been written many times) and the drive's own
state are candidates, none of them tested.

**3. The warning names the host in each of those runs.** R3, R5w and R9 each printed
`feed_ratio`'s warning: "The drive spent tape repositioning behind an irregular feed; see the
operator guide's quiet-host rule. … this is a warning about the host". In the same sweep the
drive reported 0 repositions per 100 MB, and page 0x02 showed the corrections above. The
volumes were complete and correct, as the warning says.

**4. Page 0x34's repositions figure read 0 in every run, the 1.094 included.** Parameters 1 to
4 decode to sensible figures, so the decoder reads the page. They show 157.3 MB/s falling to
155.4 MB/s into the buffer, a maximum of 800 MB/s, and a native 160 MB/s. The 0 is the drive's
own reading: under one direction change per 100 MB, which is the parameter's floor
(`HpPerformance` in `src/tape/log_pages.rs` says so).

P0's question has its answer for the rate parameters: **an average since load, not a recent
window.** Parameters 1 and 3 drifted by 0 to 3 units a run across the whole phase. R9's write,
paced at 140 MB/s, moved parameter 1 from 1556 to 1554. A recent window would have dropped it
toward 1400. For parameter 0 the phase gives no answer, because it never left 0. §11's "a
wear figure at no cost" holds only for a write whose repositions reach the floor. A drive fed
as these were does not reach it in 20 GB.

**5. ring removes the hasher as the bottleneck.** With `sha2` the writer waited 83-104 s of a
write of 135-163 s, and the write ran at 131.6-148.7 MB/s. With `ring` (R8) it waited 16 s
and ran at 158.9 MB/s, about the drive's native 160. R8 also had the 4 GiB queue and the
cross-file carry, and R6 (the queue alone) did not run, so the queue's share is not separated.
The 1.6.0 acceptance's write used ring with today's 256 MiB queue and averaged 160 MB/s over
2.28 TiB, which leaves the queue little to add.

**6. The pace made nothing better.** R9 (ring, pace 140) gave 1.0154 against R8's 1.0021 under
the same load, and its write ran at 134.5 MB/s. Its writer never waited, so the pace was the
only limit. R9's excess came with a correction jump like R3's and R5w's. One run of each
cannot say whether the pace caused it.

## The restore leg

`shp-restore` (1.5 GiB, 2 slices) was restored from SHP-11 by `tapectl restore unit` with the
research build, then by RESTORE.sh with age v1.3.2. Each tree matched the source's checksum
(`27afa380…`).

| Path | Wall time | Phases | Rate |
|---|---|---|---|
| `tapectl restore unit` | 117 s | drive-open 1m 23s (waiting on the rewind), read 33.0 s | read 46.5 MiB/s; 13.1 MiB/s over the wall time |
| RESTORE.sh `--restore` | 219 s | not broken down: ID thunk, envelope, two slices, dar, per-file sha256 check | 7.0 MiB/s over the wall time |

- **A release build's restore is not CPU-starved.** The 1.6.0 acceptance's debug build decrypted
  at 3.0 MiB/s, and RESTORE.sh was four times faster there. In a release build tapectl's read
  phase ran at 46.5 MiB/s, and RESTORE.sh took nearly twice tapectl's wall time.
- **Where the read phase's time goes is not known.** 46.5 MiB/s is under a third of the drive's
  rate, and slice 1 alone (1 GiB) took 22.5 s. The read path has no pipeline trace. Decryption,
  hashing, dar's extraction and md3's writes are all candidates, none of them measured.
- **The 83 s rewind** is the tape coming back from where SHP-11's write left it. A restore
  straight after a write pays it.
